<b>W2.</b><br>
You have a preliminary meeting to discuss some of the features for the SMS app, and one of the senior members of the team suggests a solution that you strongly believe will not work. What, if any, actions would you take?<br>

<b>Answer</b><br>
If I really think the solution won’t work, I would have to say something as keeping quiet may only make things worse, or validate the problem later down the line when it it's more expensive to fix. What matters is the way I would say it, as it requires respect and understanding.
First, I’d make sure I’ve actually understood what they’re suggesting. I’d ask a couple of questions like ‘How would that handle it if the SMS provider times out?’ or ‘What happens if we need to send to a few hundred thousand customers at once?’
Sometimes they’ve already thought it through and I’ve missed something. If so, I’ve learned something because of my questioning. And if there is a gap, asking it that way often brings it out without it turning into me versus them.
If I still think it won’t work, I'd continue to put down my concerns, keeping it about the problem rather than the person. Something like, ‘My concern with this is X, because I’ve seen Y happen before.’ Ideally, I’d back that up with something concrete like how the API behaves, rate limits, cost, or something that went wrong on a past project.
If it doesn’t get settled in the meeting, I wouldn’t keep pushing it there. I’d suggest we take it offline, or do a quick spike or proof of concept to test both approaches. That way the data decides rather than whoever’s most senior or loudest in the room.
Then, if the team still decides to go with their approach after hearing me out, I’d get behind it and help make it work. I’d make sure my concerns were noted somewhere, say on the ticket or in the design doc, so if the issue does come up later we can deal with it quickly rather than pointing fingers. I believe this way, I uphold a healthy enviromnent around the team while providing my concerns legitimately.
