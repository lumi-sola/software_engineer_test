<b>W1.</b><br>
You have taken the lead on this project, which currently has the highest priority on our backlog. However, the product owner of another app you maintain has contacted you personally and requested the creation of new features, as well as a bug fix. You feel that it would be challenging to complete all of this work within a reasonable timeframe. How would you handle this situation?<br>
<br>

<b>Answer</b><br>
The first thing I would do is not say yes straight away. It is very easy to appease someone and say yes, giving them what they want to hear, but being overcommitted to implmentations can cause cracks in production.
I would tell the product owner I have received their request and that I will look at it and get back to them by the end of the day. Then I would take a look at the bug first, because that is the part that could actually be urgent. If it is something live that is affecting customers, it may take priority to solve compared to whatever is going on. If it is more of a small hinderance, it can wait.

New features - I would treat as normal tasks. I’d get them written up as tickets, put a rough estimate on them, and get them onto the backlog properly.
After that I would speak to my manager, because my current project is top priority and it's not my call to move that around on my own. I would go to them with options, as I like to present solutions to a problem rather than just the problem itself. For example: ‘If I pick up the bug this week, the main project slips by a couple of days. The features could probably start next sprint. Or could someone else take the bug?’ Then we can decide together.
Once an agreement has been made, I would go back to the product owner and give them an honest timeline. It's better to be honest and say "not this week" rather than say "Yes", and nothing happens.
